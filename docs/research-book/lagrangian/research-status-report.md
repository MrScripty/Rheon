# Chapter titles and progression

1. **30 Material motion, connected meshes and conservation.** Defines material/spatial descriptions, motion map, deformation gradient and Jacobian, material derivatives, mass, momentum and energy. Affine stretch, shear and rigid rotation establish what moving with matter does and does not guarantee.
2. **31 Stress, constitutive laws and weak forms.** Derives Nanson's formula, Cauchy/Piola stresses, objectivity, a specified neo-Hookean energy and its tangent, Newtonian stress, essential/natural boundary conditions, action/Euler–Lagrange mechanics, and total/updated Lagrangian descriptions.
3. **32 Finite-element spaces, quadrature and element operators.** Constructs P1/P2 tetrahedral bases, reference/physical maps, consistent and lumped mass, internal force, loads and tangent assembly. Includes an exact P1 mass/shear-force example and conditional internal force/torque cancellation.
4. **33 Time integration, Newton iteration and linear solvers.** Derives central difference, backward Euler and Newmark updates, a conditional energy inequality, feasible line search, stopping/failure and Krylov/preconditioner choices. Includes an exact one-dimensional implicit step.
5. **34 Incompressibility, locking and material-mesh fluids.** Explains mixed stability/gauges and specifies a candidate P2-motion/velocity, P1-pressure Newtonian-fluid timestep, endpoint volume moments and all geometry-dependent Jacobian blocks. Distinguishes volume constraints from endpoint divergence.
6. **35 Contact, fluid–solid coupling and changing meshes.** Derives gap/complementarity and penalty equations, interface power cancellation, a scalar added-mass model, ALE/GCL identities and conservative remap properties. Separates connected FEM/PFEM from SPH/PBF/PIC/FLIP/APIC/MPM.
7. **36 Complete reference FEM solver and verification program** is the preserved checkpoint title. The revised heading is **Reference FEM algorithms and verification program**, to avoid implying an executed or fully qualified solver. It connects initialization, element evaluation, nonlinear/linear solution, geometry rejection and publication. Hydrostatics and a mixed algebra example supplement the solid calculations. Its “complete” claim is limited to end-to-end core dataflow for the selected models; missing numerical selections and broader physics are not supplied by that word.

The sequence teaches fundamentals before algorithms and treats fluid shear as rate-dependent dissipation, rather than reusing solid strain energy under a new name. Its total/updated Lagrangian and ALE sections also retain their distinct coordinate and transport meanings.

# What the independent review actually checked

A separate read-only mathematical/source reviewer checked the following by symbolic reasoning and hand arithmetic: the motion/Jacobian identities; stress transformations and the neo-Hookean derivative; P1 tetrahedral mass and shear forces; the negative P2 vertex mass row sum; backward-Euler coefficients and energy arithmetic; fluid pressure/volume tangent signs; the endpoint divergence/volume counterexample; the restrictive geometry-bound proof; interface adjoint work; ALE/GCL identities; conservative projection properties; and the conditional linear Céa argument. The hydrostatic example's signs and 9,810 Pa bottom pressure were also checked.

The reviewer caught an initial forward/backward geometry inconsistency. For endpoint backward Euler with trace-zero spatial velocity gradient diag(a,−a,0), the affine Jacobian is 1/(1−h²a²), giving 4/3 at ha=1/2. A forward update with that gradient on the old geometry instead gives 1−h²a²=3/4. The manuscript now derives and labels both. The reviewer also requested explicit nonconvergence rejection in fluid pseudocode and the conditional angular-momentum guarantee for exact Galerkin remap; both were corrected.

These are **checked derivations**, not machine-checked proofs. The conditions are essential: invertible maps; adequate regularity; exact real arithmetic/integration where claimed; positive mass; appropriate constraints; a convexity condition for the backward-Euler energy bound; and a convex reference body with a restrictive displacement-gradient bound for the simple injectivity proof. The linear Céa argument is not a nonlinear free-surface/contact convergence proof. No new FEM result has a Lean theorem, proof audit or floating-point certificate.

The reviewer also independently inspected selected pinned source files and license authorities. That supports architecture descriptions and literal source facts, not the full scientific correctness of each project. It does not verify unread paper contents. The solid and fluid algorithms are **pseudocode**, not compiled, executed or numerically validated implementations. Hand examples are not run evidence. No scientific companion, native application, GPU/hardware experiment or full scientific book build was run.

# Claim-by-claim treatment of unread full text

**L2, high-order compressible hydrodynamics.** Chapter 30 cites the paper as historical provenance, and chapter 34 gives an abstract shared-force power cancellation. The latter follows directly by multiplying the written momentum/internal-energy equations and cancelling transpose products; it does not use an unread theorem. Laghos v3.1 README/operator declarations directly substantiate its compressible Euler model, kinematic/thermodynamic spaces and shared force architecture. No polynomial-order convergence, shock accuracy or fully discrete conservation theorem from the unread paper is adopted. The attribution is narrowed accordingly.

**L4, Newmark.** Chapter 33's family equations and linear stability statement are standard mathematics, not a quotation or attributed proof from the unread 1959 body. The revised chapter provides a short independent average-acceleration/modal argument: the linear undamped method is trapezoidal on the first-order system, with modal factors (1+i h omega/2)/(1−i h omega/2) of unit modulus. Taylor expansion gives second order. This conditional reasoning needs constant symmetric positive-definite mass and symmetric positive-definite stiffness on the constrained displacement space and consistent acceleration states; nonlinear contact claims are excluded. The citation supplies date/title/provenance only.

**L5, 2004 PFEM overview.** No chapter equation or technical guarantee relies on its unread body. All technical PFEM assertions used in the draft are supported instead by the already-read 2013 authors' chapter L6, especially the connected mesh, reconstruction sequence, FIC stabilization and pressure-sign convention. The 2004 entry remains a bibliographic pointer. This is not a retry of the timed-out source or a claim that the two articles are identical.

**L7 and L8, ALE.** Chapter 35 derives the material/mesh derivative by the chain rule and moving-volume balance by Reynolds' theorem. These derivations require no unread paper body. # Coverage audit: fluids and solids are present, research closure is incomplete

This is the **M2 checkpoint inventory**, retained for traceability. M3 closes the selected compressible isentropic gas, exact quadrature and numerical-selection gaps and adds continuous ALE fluxes, discrete triangle capillary derivatives and fixed-interface coupling blocks. The supplement distinguishes those completed pieces from the remaining general solvers; do not read this historical list as an omission of the new chapters.

**Solid core:** the path reaches a specified hyperelastic law, consistent residual/tangent, P1 tetrahedral assembly, explicit/implicit updates and bounded Newton dataflow. It does not give a complete plastic/viscoelastic material integrator, fracture, calibrated material study, general nonconvex large-deformation geometry certificate, or full updated-Lagrangian element implementation. The simple geometry guard intentionally rejects many valid large deformations. The exposition therefore supports a selected reference core, not arbitrary solids.

**Incompressible fluid core:** the path reaches Newtonian stress, a mixed material-mesh residual, all geometric tangent blocks, volume-moment/gauge distinctions, free-traction boundary interpretation, hydrostatics and nonlinear solve dataflow. Important unresolved work is proof or numerical qualification of the actual moving P2/P1 pair and mesh family, pressure/traction convergence, local density/volume errors, finite-step pressure work and mesh robustness. The endpoint-volume formulation is an independently derived candidate; it is not identified as an established validated method merely because related mixed methods exist.

**Compressible Lagrangian fluids:** the material balance/internal-energy equations and a shared-force power identity are present, with Laghos architecture pointers. A complete thermodynamic FE discretization, equation of state, artificial viscosity/entropy treatment, boundary fluxes, shock handling and executable step are missing. Existing chapters 26–28 discuss a different compressible finite-volume reference and do not fill this material-mesh gap. This is the largest remaining gap in the requested fluid-to-full-solver progression.

**ALE and transfer:** chain-rule/GCL identities and conditional conservative transfer proofs are present. A general 3D relative-flux FE transport residual, compatible time/face integration, robust overlap construction, constrained transfer and a composed momentum/energy/error analysis are not fully specified. Rheon's existing restricted fitted-height/ALE studies are explicitly not a general substitute.

**Free surfaces, contact and FSI:** natural traction, surface-energy interpretation, complementarity, penalty tangent and interface adjoint-work equations are present. Full capillary curvature/surface Hessian assembly, moving contact-line laws, mortar/active-set/IPC algorithms, monolithic coupled blocks and a convergent partitioned scheme remain extensions. Topology-changing breakup/merging, remeshing and material history transfer are not complete solvers in these chapters.

**Numerics and proofs:** the draft explains locking/inf-sup, Krylov choices, Schur structure and failure modes. It does not construct a uniformly stable mixed mesh family, derive a particular stabilization/preconditioner completely, specify every quadrature node/weight and dimensional tolerance, prove nonlinear convergence or supply manufactured moving-fluid refinement results. Implementers must still select and qualify those numerical details. Linear identities and worked cases do not close these gaps.
